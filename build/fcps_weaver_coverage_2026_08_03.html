Independent firm reveals findings of FCPS audit

Weaver recommends more than 70 actions to ‘strengthen budgeting and spending controls’
By WKYT News Staff
Published: Aug. 3, 2026 at 5:41 PM EDT|Updated: 2 hours ago
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

LEXINGTON, Ky. (WKYT) - An independent firm tasked with conducting an audit of Fayette County Public Schools has released its findings.

The board held its August planning meeting virtually Monday evening. The meeting began at 5:30 p.m. at the John D. Price Building, where members of the public may attend in person to listen and participate in public comment.

Short-term loan, audit presentation on agenda

At the end of the school year, the board approved a short-term loan to cover expenses until tax revenue is collected in the fall. The district originally sought a loan of up to $110 million but reduced the amount to up to $95 million, citing increased cash flow.

Agenda items from the meeting state the financial assessment presented at the July action meeting remains valid and relatively unchanged. The unaudited annual financial report has been submitted to the Kentucky Department of Education.

District leaders said more cuts will be needed in the upcoming budget.

The board is expected to go into closed session at the end of Monday’s meeting. The next FCPS action meeting is scheduled for Aug. 17 at 6 p.m.

Based on the Weaver presentation slides regarding the Fayette County Public Schools (FCPS) Audit of Budget Processes and Expenditures, here is a summary of the audit’s scope, procedures, timeline, and key findings:

1. Audit Scope & Objectives

Weaver was engaged to perform an independent internal audit focusing on two core workstreams:

  • Budget Process Review: Evaluation of end-to-end stages (Draft, Tentative, Working Budget, and post-adoption monitoring) including forecasting, oversight, roles, and communication.
  • Travel and Event Expense Review: Analysis of governance, policy compliance, supporting documentation, and transaction data over travel/event expenditures and school P-Cards.
  • Data-Driven Variance Analysis: In-depth analytics of the General Fund to pinpoint root causes of budget-to-actual variances and anomaly detection.

2. Project Timeline & Phase

  • Oct–Nov 2025: Project RFP & Award
  • Dec 2025–Jan 2026: Project Planning
  • Feb 2026: Initial Interviews
  • Mar–May 2026: Document Gathering, Analysis, and Testing
  • Jun 2026: Preliminary Result Drafts Begin
  • Jul 2026: Final Report & Recommendations Delivered

3. Executive Summary & Major High-Level Metrics

  • ~1% Reserve Level: The FY2025 ending General Fund reserve ended at roughly 1% of spending, falling below both the 2% state minimum threshold and the district’s own 6% policy guideline.
  • 6 Core Themes: Identified with major risks.
  • 70+ Recommended Actions: Proposed to strengthen budgeting and control processes.
  • 10 Top Priority Actions: Set on a phased 18-month roadmap to move the district toward a more reliable, transparent budget.

4. Summary of Results – Six Core Themes

The audit consolidated its findings and highlighted major vulnerabilities across six areas:

  1. Documentation & Audit Trail: Budget amendments lacked documented justification. The district lacked consistent retention of receipts, approvals, and business-purpose documentation for purchases.
  2. Policies & Internal Controls: Written policies are outdated, incomplete, or absent. For instance, travel and accounting handbook guidelines lack enforcement, and standardizing review procedures is necessary to reduce compliance risks.
  3. Roles & Management Review: Oversight suffers from a lack of evidence of independent reviews. Review systems often allow single-user approval power on key transfers, requiring a clear segregation of duties.
  4. Reconciliation & Monitoring: Lack of formal budget monitoring frameworks. Expense reviews are not consistently performed to identify unrecorded or misclassified expenses.
  5. Coding & Classification Accuracy: Revenues and expenses are frequently recorded under incorrect or inconsistent object codes, leading to unreliable budget-to-actual comparisons.
  6. Governance, Board Oversight, and Transparency: Budget amendments and inter-fund transfers are executed without the Board’s formal, up-front approval. Policies need explicit criteria on when the Board must approve budget changes.

The Fayette County Public Schools general fund is projected to end fiscal year 2026 in a deficit, according to the district’s unaudited annual financial report. The district spent $10 million more than its $700 million projected revenue.

The district also confirmed that Deputy Superintendent Houston Barber retired on July 31. A month before his planned retirement.
