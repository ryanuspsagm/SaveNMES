We went through the state's grade-by-grade files for North Middletown Elementary, three years of them, to answer the question everyone is asking: what happened this year?

The headline is bad and we are not dressing it up. In 2025-26, 21 percent of NMES students were proficient in reading, down from 41 percent. Math fell from 31 to 23. The state has named NMES a Comprehensive Support and Improvement school, one of 53 in Kentucky.

Here is what the record underneath shows.

About 13 of the 20 reading points come from a swap, not a slide: a strong fifth grade (54 percent proficient) moved on to middle school and a weak third grade (18 percent) moved in. In math that swap is the whole story. The children who were at NMES both years actually improved in math on net.

The other 7 points sit in one room. This year's fifth graders were the county's best fourth grade a year ago: 47 percent proficient in reading, 41 in math, 53 in science. A year later every fifth-grade subject fell. Cohorts at the other county schools moved a few points between fourth and fifth grade; this one fell off a cliff. One classroom, one year, every subject points at the room, not the school. The staffing record that would explain it is not public yet.

The fourth grade got better. The third grade is the slow problem: NMES third graders have come in weaker three years running while the district's third grade rose. That is a K-2 question, and it predates this year.

And no, it is not children leaving. The tested cohorts held between the two tests: 20, 26 and 19 children in October 2024, and 19, 24 and 19 a year on.

What the CSI label means, from KDE's own rules: a state audit of the school and the district within 45 days, a three-year turnaround plan, federal school improvement money, state staff on site, and the school council's authority moving to the superintendent for the turnaround. A school exits when it climbs out of the bottom five percent and improves. It is not a closure order. The law assumes the school stays open and gets help.

The district is now deciding whether to close a school the state has just placed on a three-year improvement track. A turnaround plan and a closure plan cannot both be the district's plan for the same building.

Every number here traces to a state file you can open yourself at savenmes.org.
