## 3. Filling in the suppressed cells from the school totals

KDE suppresses a grade when a performance level holds fewer than three children, but it still publishes the whole-school figure, and the grade sizes are public (SAAR end-of-year counts: 22, 17 and 26 in grades 3 to 5 in 2024-25; 19, 24 and 19 in 2025-26). Subtracting the published grades from the school total brackets the suppressed one.

## 7. Reading

- **Not one bad cohort, and not a school-wide slide either: it is two separate things at once.** Swapping the departing fifth grade (54 percent proficient in reading, 46 in math) for the incoming third grade (18 and 14) accounts for about 13 of the 20 reading points and for all of the math fall; in math the continuing cohorts actually improved on net. The rest of the reading fall sits in the fifth grade.
- **The fifth grade of 2025-26 is the anomaly.** Those children scored 47 percent proficient in reading and 41 in math as fourth graders in 2025, with 53 percent proficient in science, the best fourth grade in the county. A year later the bracketing puts them near 25 to 31 percent in math and the fourth-and-fifth-grade pair at 21 to 23 percent in reading, while editing and mechanics fell to 16 percent against 50 and 52 for the two previous fifth grades, and the writing and social studies indexes fell from 77 and 47 to 25 and 22. Cohorts at the comparison schools moved by at most seven points between fourth and fifth grade. A drop of this size, in every subject, in one classroom-year, points at that classroom: a teacher change, a long-term substitute, or a disrupted year. The public record cannot yet say which; the 2025-26 turnover figure will.
- **The fourth grade did well.** The same cohort that was suppressed as third graders in 2025 (bracketed at 3 to 8 percent proficient in math) reached 26 percent in math as fourth graders, a larger gain than any comparison school's cohort made. Whatever went wrong in 2025-26, it did not go wrong in every room.
- **The entry grade is the slow problem.** NMES's third graders have come in weaker three years running, 29 percent proficient in reading in 2024 (a class of 15), about 20 percent in 2025, 18 percent in 2026 with 64 percent novice, while the district's third grade rose from 30 to 38. That is a kindergarten-through-second-grade question, of instruction or of who enrolls: the 2026 third graders were the first-grade class of 2023-24, which carried 8 homeless children of 27 and 8 chronically absent. It is the one systemic signal in the file, and it predates this year.
- **Children leaving does not explain 2025-26.** NMES lost 17 children between October 2024 and June 2025 (12 percent, against 1 to 7 percent at the other three schools), mostly in kindergarten, first and third grade, before the strong spring 2025 tests. Between the two tests the tested cohorts held or grew: the children in grades 2, 3 and 4 in October 2024 numbered 20, 26 and 19, and those same cohorts numbered 19, 24 and 19 at the end of 2025-26. Net departures were small. What the public file cannot show is churn, leavers replaced by arrivals, which would matter most in the fifth-grade room; that is a records question.
- **What else is ruled out.** Participation was 100 percent in both prior years. Every NMES teacher in 2024-25 was experienced. Turnover into 2024-25 was 2 of 9. Chronic absenteeism in the tested grades was moderate (6, 3 and 5 children). Retention was zero. The district's own grades were flat, so it is not the test.

## 8. What would settle the fifth-grade question

Records request, location 090: the certified staff roster for 2024-25 and 2025-26 by grade assignment with separation dates and any long-term substitute assignments; withdrawals and new enrollments by grade and month for 2025-26; chronic absenteeism by grade for 2025-26; and the district's fall, winter and spring benchmark results for the fifth grade. The 2025-26 School Report Card, due later this fall, will publish teacher turnover and absenteeism without a request.
