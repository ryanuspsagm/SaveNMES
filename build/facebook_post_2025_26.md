# Facebook post: the 2025-26 scores and the CSI label

---

The state released this year's test scores and school ratings on October 14. We promised to share the numbers whatever they showed. This year they are bad, and we are not going to spin them.

On the 2025-26 Kentucky Summative Assessment, North Middletown Elementary scored lowest of the county's elementary schools in every subject (tied in science). Reading: 21 percent of students proficient or distinguished, down from 41 percent last year. Math: 23 percent, down from 31. The state withheld the science, social studies and writing percentages because the classes are so small, but its index score for each of those fell too.

Kentucky has named NMES a Comprehensive Support and Improvement school, one of 53 in the state and the only one in Bourbon County. That means its overall score landed in the bottom five percent of Kentucky elementary schools.

The chart shows this year's "status" score by subject, the score the state builds its ratings from. It leaves out the state's "change" piece. Kentucky scores a school for where it landed this year and then again for how far it fell from last year, which is how NMES's official overall score came out at 18.2 when its status-only score is 39.1. On status alone NMES sits right at the state's five percent line. Either way it is a bad year, and one year earlier the same school was first in the county in every subject. That swing is what a school of about 70 tested children looks like: one fifth-grade class carries two of the five subjects.

What CSI actually means, from KDE's own rules:
- KDE audits the school and the district within 45 days.
- A turnaround team writes a three-year improvement plan, running by fall 2027.
- The school council's authority moves to the superintendent during the turnaround.
- The school becomes eligible for federal school improvement money and on-site state support.
- A school exits when it climbs out of the bottom five percent and improves its overall score.

What it does not mean: it is not a closure order. The law assumes the school stays open, run by the district, with state help and money attached. The district is now deciding whether to close a school the state has just placed on a three-year improvement track.

Our ask has not changed: keep NMES permanent in the facility plan, give the school the four years to the next plan to grow with public targets, and let the community raise money for the building. The full numbers, the state's files and the CSI documents are at savenmes.org.

Source: Kentucky Department of Education, Assessment and Accountability Data 2025-2026, released October 14, 2026; KDE, Comprehensive Support and Improvement Process (KRS 160.346 and 703 KAR 5:280).
